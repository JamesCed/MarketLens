How accurate is this? Partly measured, partly modelled — and the map labels which is which. Competitor counts for a barangay this system has actually queried are real businesses returned by the Google Places API. Population and population density are the real 2024 PSA census figures. Everything else is an estimate: where no live lookup has been made for an (industry × barangay) pair, the count is generated from the surrounding data so the model still has something to score, and it is flagged as estimated wherever it appears.

Why synthetic data is used at all. There is no real-time feed of business registrations for Tarlac City, and a complete sweep of all 20 industries across all 76 barangays is thousands of billable API calls — beyond the scope of a student project. So the gaps are filled with generated figures. That is a deliberate trade: it makes the system complete enough to demonstrate and evaluate end to end, while every generated value is replaced by a real one the moment that combination is actually fetched. The design improves as real data accumulates rather than needing to be rebuilt. Treat the shaded map as an indication of where to look, not as a survey.

About the boundaries. The bold blue outline is Tarlac City's real administrative border. The lines inside it are not: no per-barangay boundary dataset is published for Tarlac City, only point coordinates, so each shaded cell is the area closer to that barangay's own surveyed coordinate than to any other's (see app/services/choropleth_service.py). A cell's internal edge is therefore a computed approximation; its outer edge stops exactly at the real city border. Per-barangay figures in the detail panel and location list are exact regardless. Household income is not published per barangay in the Philippines (see Reference/DATASETS.md), which is why population density is the sociodemographic overlay offered here.

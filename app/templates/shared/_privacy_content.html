MarketLens processes personal information in line with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173), its Implementing Rules and Regulations, and the issuances of the National Privacy Commission (NPC), following the principles of transparency, legitimate purpose and proportionality.

1. Who is responsible

The LGU that runs MarketLens is the personal information controller; the MarketLens development team processes data on its behalf. Questions, requests and complaints go to {% include "shared/_dpo_contact.html" %}.

2. What we collect

  • Account details: name, e-mail address, gender, birthday, contact number, address, and your role (business owner or LGU official).
  • Sign-in credentials: your password (stored only as a hash, see section 4) and the one-time codes we e-mail you.
  • Business plans: business name, industry, barangay, capital, staff, prices and ideas you enter, and the forecasts computed for them.
  • Location queries: the industry and barangay you choose when you plan, search, or open the map. MarketLens never asks for your device’s GPS location.
  • Security records: an audit trail of significant actions (what was done, when, from which page, IP address and browser), and failed sign-in attempts.
  • Human-verification signals: when a form is protected by Cloudflare Turnstile, Cloudflare checks browser and network signals to tell people from bots and returns only a pass/fail token.

3. Why we use it

  • to create and secure your account and keep you signed in;
  • to produce your forecasts, recommendations and alerts, and the city-wide figures the LGU plans with;
  • to keep the service safe: stopping bots, brute-force sign-ins and abuse, and investigating incidents.

We rely on your consent and the Terms you accept at sign-up (RA 10173, Sec. 12(a) and (b)) and, for records the LGU uploads, on the LGU’s public functions (Sec. 12(e)). Your data is never sold and never used for advertising.

4. How your credentials and session are protected

  • Passwords are hashed, never stored. Each password is turned into a salted one-way hash (scrypt; accounts created before October 2026 use PBKDF2-SHA256 until the password is next changed). Nobody — not administrators, not the developers — can read your password, and it is never sent by e-mail.
  • Sessions use a signed cookie, not a JSON Web Token. MarketLens does not issue JWTs, API keys or other bearer tokens to users. After you sign in, a session cookie signed with a server-side secret identifies you. It is HttpOnly (page scripts cannot read it), Secure (sent only over HTTPS) and SameSite=Lax (not sent with requests from other sites), and it ends when you sign out. “Remember me” adds a separate signed cookie that keeps you signed in on that device until you sign out.
  • Forms are protected by anti-forgery (CSRF) tokens, and every page checks your role on the server.
  • One-time codes for e-mail verification and password reset expire after a few minutes and allow only a few attempts.
  • Brute force is slowed down: repeated failed sign-ins are blocked for a while, and sign-in, sign-up and password reset are protected by invisible human verification.
  • Personal details are encrypted at rest: gender, birthday, contact number and address are encrypted in the database (AES with an authentication code), with a key kept outside the database. Administrators see them masked.

5. How location queries are used

  • To count competitors, the server — not your device — asks the Google Places API for businesses of an industry in a barangay (for example, bakeries in San Roque, Tarlac City). The query contains the industry and the barangay only: never your name, account or location. What comes back is kept as counts per industry and barangay.
  • The Saturation Map’s tiles are loaded by your browser from OpenStreetMap’s servers, which, like any website, see your IP address.
  • Barangay choices in your plans are used to score those plans. City-wide figures that the LGU sees are totals, never your individual plan or identity.

6. Government data uploaded by LGU accounts

LGU accounts upload barangay zoning figures and business permit lists. From a permit list only each permit’s barangay, industry, line of business, status and permit number are read. Owner names, contact numbers, e-mail and home addresses, TIN and other ID numbers and birth dates must not be included — a file that contains them is refused. Only totals are kept: one row per barangay and counts of permits in force per industry and kind of business, with the uploading account and the date. The file is deleted as soon as it has been processed; no business name, permit number or row of the file is stored. Business owners see only the resulting scores and counts per barangay.

Uploads follow a separation of duties. An LGU Encoder’s upload first goes to a staging area, where an LGU Data Steward reviews it and approves or rejects it, and only a System Administrator publishes approved data to the live dataset. Staged totals are deleted when the batch is published or rejected; a batch not published within 60 days expires and its totals are deleted. Each step is recorded in the audit trail.

6a. Verifying LGU staff and officials

LGU staff register with their name, official work e-mail address and password, and tell us their jurisdiction (city-wide or one barangay), their department or office, their official job title, the LGU role they need and a short reason. Nothing else is asked: no birthday, address, contact number or ID. The account stays “pending verification” until the System Administrator approves or rejects the request, and the decision is sent to the work e-mail address. The office, job title, jurisdiction and role stay on the account while it holds LGU access; the request itself (with the reason) is seen only by administrators and is deleted with the audit trail once it is decided.

7. Who receives it

  • Within MarketLens: you see your own plans; LGU officials see city-wide totals; administrators manage accounts and see your name and e-mail, with other details masked.
  • Service providers that run parts of the system for us, each only for its task: Render (web hosting), Aiven (database hosting), Brevo or Gmail (sending e-mails: your address and name), Google (Places counts and Gemini explanations: no personal details), Cloudflare (human verification). Some of them process data outside the Philippines, under their own security and privacy terms.
  • Authorities, only when the law requires it.

8. How long it is kept

  • Account details and plans: while your account exists. Plans in Trash are deleted after 30 days.
  • Audit trail entries: {{ kept_audit_days }} days, then deleted automatically.
  • LGU uploads: {{ kept_upload_days }} days after upload, then deleted automatically. Staged uploads waiting for review or publishing: at most 60 days.
  • LGU access requests: until the System Administrator decides, then as long as the audit trail.
  • Uploaded files: deleted right after processing.

9. If something goes wrong

If a personal data breach that the law requires us to report occurs, the National Privacy Commission and the people affected are notified within 72 hours of its discovery, as NPC Circular 16-03 requires, with what happened, what data was involved, what is being done and whom to contact.

10. Your rights

Under RA 10173 you have the right to be informed, to access your data, to object to its processing, to have it corrected, erased or blocked, to data portability, to damages, and to file a complaint with the National Privacy Commission (privacy.gov.ph). You can correct most details yourself in Settings; for anything else contact {% include "shared/_dpo_contact.html" %}.

11. Cookies

MarketLens uses only the cookies it needs to work: the session cookie, the optional “remember me” cookie and the anti-forgery token. There are no advertising or analytics cookies.
